# Try a Day: open items to review and revise

Try a Day is a career exploration app for students, with separate sections for parents and guardians and for teachers. This file lists what still needs checking, what we parked for later, and what the school's tech team needs to know.

- **Live version:** https://claude.ai/artifact/FFDKy5hgW3WWyrb696hpag (updates whenever changes are published)
- **School copy:** `try-a-day.html`, one self-contained file. It is a snapshot, so request a new copy after any change.

## 1. Review with the counselor's office

I wrote these from general knowledge, not official sources. Each needs a check before students and families rely on it.

### Student content
- [ ] **Training steps for all 51 careers.** Each career has three steps: junior year, senior year and after graduation. The labels change with the student's grade.
- [ ] **"Good to know" application tips**, one per career.
- [ ] **Classes, clubs and high school types by career group** (8 groups), shown to middle school, 9th and 10th grade students. The counselor may want to swap in programs your district actually offers.
- [ ] **Program descriptions** in "Explore the world and your interests" (12 programs) and "Pay for college and get ready" (8 programs). Check eligibility wording, especially SEO Scholars (which areas it serves) and QuestBridge (income and academic requirements).
- [ ] **The 21 careers marked "New to me."** I chose which careers count as lesser-known.
- [ ] **Which career group each career belongs to.** This drives browsing and the class and program suggestions.

### Parent and guardian content
- [ ] **Adult next steps.** These are written by training type (apprenticeship, short program, college, graduate school), not job by job.
- [ ] **"How you can help" tips for parents**, also written by training type.
- [ ] **Typical tasks for 32 common jobs** that aren't career pages, such as cashier, CNA, office manager and stay-at-home parent.
- [ ] **Task guesses from 17 job-title keywords**, such as "manager," "driver" or "technician."
- [ ] **The 18 everyday activities** in the parent "narrow it down" path, and the skills each one maps to.
- [ ] **"Similar jobs" suggestions** for jobs the app doesn't have (104 search terms). For example, "chef" or "pharmacist" suggests related jobs the app does have.

### Teacher content (IEP transition prep, added November 26, 2026)
Review these with the special education team, ideally the transition coordinator.
- [ ] **Interview questions** for each Level 1 area: vocational interests, academic strengths and functional skills, learning style, and work-related behaviors, plus the answer choices for each.
- [ ] **Draft sentence templates** for the Level 1 table, PLOP student strengths, measurable postsecondary goals, transition needs and the coordinated set of transition activities. They follow the NYSED *Guide to Quality IEP Development and Implementation* (revised September 2023). Goals are written as "After high school, [student] will…" Transition needs and activities are written with school staff acting ("[student] will be instructed in…", "will be provided with…").
- [ ] **Interview order (updated):** directions (with a required diploma choice: Local, Regents or Advanced Regents) → the 3 intake questions → "Do you know what career you want?" → try a day. Once a job has been typed in or one job has been looked at, the teacher can skip straight to the drafts.
- [ ] **Teacher intake answers:** in the teacher interview, "What does a good day feel like?" and "What matters most in a job?" allow any number of answers (students and parents keep their limits of 2 and 1). If more than one is picked and the comment box is empty, the teacher is prompted to ask a narrowing follow-up question. Only the first "matters most" answer is used to rank job matches; all of them appear in the draft.
- [ ] **Career not in Try a Day:** a card right under the search bar says the named career still goes in the vocational assessment, and suggests trying a day in a related job. "In Try a Day" means every word the teacher typed starts a word in a job's name (so "vet tech" counts as Veterinary technician, but "marine biologist" does not count as Wildlife biologist).
- [ ] **Student report details in the Level 1 (added November 26, 2026):** the vocational interests box now ends with career details like the student report (typical pay, training and key skills for the student's career goal, or for their top 3 matches if they're unsure). A new **Recommended next steps** box comes before the PLOP: classes to take, clubs and activities, step-by-step plan for the career by grade, and programs to look into (with links). The PLOP gets one sentence summarizing the most important next steps, and the Education/Training goal mentions the same coursework. The same classes also feed the Transition Needs and Instruction drafts.
- [ ] **Third-person, school-action wording** in the Recommended next steps and Transition Needs drafts. Each student-voice step is rewritten from its first verb: "Take biology" becomes "Maya will be scheduled to take biology", "Apply to…" becomes "will be assisted in applying to…", "Volunteer…" becomes "will be connected with opportunities to volunteer…", and after-graduation steps become "will be prepared to…". All 127 jobs' steps were checked; review the verb list with your team.
- [ ] **Executive functioning questions** (added to work-related behaviors): follow-through on turning work in on time, remembering to complete and turn in assignments, and organization and planning. A "Developing" or "Needs support" rating adds executive functioning support to the drafts. If related services apply, it goes in the Related Services row as counseling. If not, it goes in Instruction as academic supports, or in Community Experiences if Instruction is also unchecked.
- [ ] **PLOP diploma sentence:** "[Name] plans on earning a [diploma] in order to reach their career goal of becoming [career]." If the student is unsure: "...as they continue to explore their career options. Their preferences indicate that they would be most suited for work as [top 3 matching jobs]."
- [ ] **Default "responsible" wording** in the activities table (for example, "School district: school counselor; ACCES-VR"). Swap in your district's actual titles.
- [ ] **Pay goal ranges** in the money question (under $30,000 up to more than $100,000).
- [ ] **The training document link** (osepartnership.org file 114) wouldn't open for me. I used the OSE Partnership *Family Guide to Transition Planning* (version 2.0) instead. Check that the drafts meet the standard in the training document you meant.
- [ ] **Quote rule:** anything typed in quotation marks is copied word for word, and the page warns the teacher if a quote is changed while revising. Confirm this matches how your team wants quotes used.
- [ ] **Privacy:** nothing is saved or sent. Student information stays in the browser tab and is erased by "New student" or closing the tab. Confirm this meets district policy before teachers use real student names.

## 2. Data to verify and update every year

- [ ] **Pay figures.** All 127 use U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics median pay for May 2025, rounded. One exception: heavy equipment operator uses May 2024, the latest figure found. Update them each year when BLS releases new figures.
  - These figures came from sites republishing BLS data, not bls.gov itself. Check these first: **software developer, power line worker, logistics coordinator, farm or ranch manager, animal trainer, underwater welder (commercial diver), industrial maintenance mechanic, professional athlete, musician or singer, crane operator, insurance underwriter.**
  - Several figures cover a wider BLS group, and the cards say so: police (includes detectives), paramedic (includes EMTs), ship's officer (all water transportation workers), occupational therapy assistant (includes aides).
  - **Perfumer** has no BLS category of its own; it uses the median pay for chemists, and the card says so. **Film and TV makeup artist** uses the BLS "theatrical and performance" category, a small field with a very wide pay range; the card notes that salon and bridal artists usually earn much less. **Elevator inspector** uses all construction and building inspectors. **Wind farm site manager** uses the broad BLS group "managers, all other" ($141,900), which may run high; real job ads range from about $75K to $145K. Both cards say so. Consider replacing the wind figure with a better source. **Power plant operator** uses the BLS group that includes distributors and dispatchers. **Workplace safety technician** uses technicians only ($61,560); the card mentions that specialists with a bachelor's earn more. **Musician and actor** have hourly pay only (BLS publishes no yearly figure), so their cards show pay per hour and they sort last by pay. **Military (enlisted)** uses 2026 basic pay from the DFAS pay tables plus the Congressional Research Service average for an E-5; update both each January. **Video game designer** uses the BLS group for web and digital interface designers (O*NET 15-1255.01), and the card says so. **Small business owner** shows no pay figure on purpose, because the government publishes none; the card explains why. **Hairstylist** pay ($36K) is the government's figure for hairdressers, hairstylists and cosmetologists.
- [ ] **The budget screen** (second opening screen):
  - Rent and utilities, $1,500: Census Bureau median gross rent, 2024 ($1,487). Consider using local rent instead.
  - Groceries, $350: USDA low-cost food plan, 2026, taken from a secondary source. Confirm against USDA's own table.
  - Car, $500 a month: my estimate for a used car.
  - Take-home pay: my estimate, counting federal taxes only.
- [ ] **Adult fact screens:** World Economic Forum Future of Jobs Report 2025 (39%), Department of Labor apprenticeship data for fiscal 2021 (3 in 5 apprentices 25 or older; average age about 30), and LinkedIn surveys (62% have taken a career break).
- [ ] **Program links and deadlines.** Check the links for all 20 programs once a year. Deadlines move; NSLI-Y and YoungArts both changed dates between years.
- [ ] **Gallup report statistics** are paraphrased with credit, because the report's copyright notice restricts quoting. Ask Gallup for permission before quoting it directly.

## 3. Features parked for later

1. **Credentials and expertise for adults.** "Plan for me" treats a job as everyday tasks, so it undersells people with degrees and licenses, such as a speech-language pathologist.
   - Ideas: (a) ask about highest education or license and its field, and count that schooling as done; (b) ask what they know deeply, such as health, language, children or law; (c) for licensed professionals, explain that upskilling usually means a specialty certification, supervising or private practice, and point to their professional association; (d) add professional jobs to the built-in list.
   - Recommended: (a) and (c) together.
2. **Link programs to the jobs a student saves.** Each explore program is already tagged with the career groups it fits (study abroad with travel and the world, art programs with design and creativity, and so on). Next step: show "Fits jobs you saved" and put those programs first.
3. **Grade level for parents planning for a child.** Parent pages say "Junior year" instead of assuming 11th grade. With several children, each child could have a grade.

5. **Real artwork.** The workday scenes are simple placeholder illustrations drawn in code.
6. **Local information.** Local rent on the budget screen, and the district's own high schools, CTE programs and dual enrollment.
7. **"Gamify" button for students** (requested by student testers). It would turn a career exploration into a game: students see a real situation someone in that job faces and guess what to do, for example "A storm knocked out power. As the line worker, what's your first move?" Each answer shows what a professional would actually do and why.
   - Questions to settle:
     - Where it lives: a button on each job, or its own mode.
     - Whether to keep score or award badges.
     - Whether students' choices should count as likes and dislikes in their matches.
     - Who writes or checks the scenarios. They need someone who knows the job, like the training steps do.
   - A good first step: write scenarios for 5 or 6 popular careers and test them with students before covering all 51.
8. **"I am a teacher" on the welcome page.** A teacher version to use when interviewing students for transition planning, especially vocational assessments.
   - Possible features:
     - An interview mode the teacher runs alongside the student. It would ask the same kinds of questions (good days, strengths, likes and dislikes on real job tasks) in plain language the teacher can read aloud.
     - Space for the teacher's notes and observations next to the student's answers.
     - A summary organized around the IEP transition plan's postsecondary goal areas: education or training, employment, and, where it applies, independent living. It would include the student's interests, preferences, strengths and the careers explored, so it can feed into the transition assessment.
     - Accessibility supports for students with disabilities, such as read-aloud, fewer choices per screen and picture-based options.
   - Questions to settle:
     - Which vocational assessment or district forms the summary should line up with.
     - Whether the teacher needs to save sessions. Today nothing is stored. Saving student information would bring in FERPA and district privacy rules, so a copy or email summary may be the safer start.
     - Who reviews the wording. A transition coordinator or special education teacher should check it.

## 4. Notes for the tech team

- **One self-contained file.** The only outside request is for two Google Fonts; if it's blocked, the app falls back to standard fonts.
- **Nothing is stored or sent.** No accounts, no tracking. Students copy or email their report before closing the page. If accounts are ever added, student privacy laws (COPPA, FERPA) and district rules apply.
- **Link back in emailed reports.** It points to the live claude.ai link. On the school's own website, it automatically links to that page instead. To set it by hand, edit the `SITE_URL` line in the code.
- **Email buttons** open Gmail, Outlook or the computer's email app with everything filled in. Nothing is sent until the student presses Send. "Copy the email instead" is the backup.
- **External links** to programs, Google searches and email open in a new tab.
